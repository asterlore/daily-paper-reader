# CellCanon: An Agentic Cellular World Model for Prediction, Explanation, and Observation-Driven Updating 

Zhaokang Liang ∗ Shuyang Zhuang ∗ Xiaoran Jiao Hao Chen †

Zhejiang University 

Abstract 

Biological discovery requires using molecular knowledge and new measurements to infer responses beyond the conditions already observed. We introduce Cell-Canon, the first agentic cellular world model to unify single-cell expression pre-diction, evidence-grounded explanation, and observation-driven updating. Its pre-dictive core reuses condition-level biological evidence across gene-specific numer-ical readouts to guide single-cell expression generation. A shared world state links predictions, acquired measurements, and source-linked claims, supporting expla-nation and revision. On 339 held-out SciPlex3 conditions across three cell lines, CellCanon improves differential-expression AUPRC and Macro-F1 over the best baseline for each metric by 63.3% and 17.3%, respectively, and direction Macro-F1 by 7.2%. Relative to gene-wise reasoning on matched drug–cell cohorts, mea-sured language-model token use falls by 99.77% for Predict and 98.41% for Ex-plain. On the source-grounded benchmark, Explain correctly answers all answer-able questions with supporting proofs and identifies all insufficient-evidence cases. In simulated experimental feedback, Update uses one measured source context per drug to exceed the best fine-tuned baselines in differential-expression AUPRC and Macro-F1 without changing model weights. Together, these capabilities support a cumulative account of cellular responses, with predictions and biological judg-ments that can be revised as evidence accumulates. 

1 Introduction 

Single-cell perturbation screens reveal drug-induced transcriptional changes but leave many com-pounds and cell contexts unmeasured [Srivatsan et al., 2020, Bunne et al., 2024]. A cellular world model must combine molecular knowledge and new measurements to predict unobserved responses while keeping biological judgments inspectable and revisable. Numerical perturbation models efficiently predict expression profiles [Lotfollahi et al., 2023, Hetzel et al., 2022, Piran et al., 2024, Qi et al., 2024]. Gene-wise language reasoning combines molec-ular knowledge and retrieved examples to infer response labels [Wei et al., 2026]. Genes in one drug–cell condition share biological background but require distinct response estimates. We reuse this evidence interpretation across gene-specific numerical readouts, with distinct supervision for continuous response, differential expression (DE), and conditional direction (DIR). We introduce CellCanon, the first agentic cellular world model to unify single-cell expression pre-diction, evidence-grounded explanation, and observation-driven updating. Encoded molecular evi-dence and a training-response atlas feed continuous multilayer perceptron (MLP) and classification readouts, whose gene-specific outputs guide single-cell generation (Figure 2). Single-cell generation. Matched DMSO cells, dose-dependent parameters, and the continuous and classification readouts generate single-cell expression (Figure 2). Continuous metrics score generated expression; differential testing against DMSO yields population DE/DIR (Section B.4). 

3.3 Explain: evidence-grounded reasoning and correction 

Explain assesses source support for target action, control-cell expression, regulatory paths, and source statements, returning supported , contradicted , or insufficient evidence judgments with ci-tations and natural-language rationales. Control-cell expression supplies cellular context; functional and binding records characterize drug–target interactions. Candidate drug–target–regulatory paths retain evidence types and edge signs. Tools enumerate unknown-sign assignments consistently across shared edges, and the language model selects eligible paths. Review checks citations, biological interpretation, and path consistency, providing localized diag-nostics for revision. Claims depend on background records, predictions, or measurements: new measurements can make claims answerable; source withdrawal can remove support. Section F spec-ifies the source-question and observation-state tasks. 

3.4 Update: incorporating newly measured responses 

Update uses a newly measured drug response to revise predictions for the same drug in unmeasured cell contexts. The environment reveals a selected source condition’s response and DE/DIR labels. 4A support-trained kernel ridge model combines measured source-response similarity with chemical and biological similarities to infer the target response (Equations (12) and (13)). The parent predictor and update maps remain fixed. One or two source-cell responses can be used; without a source measurement, the initial prediction is retained. Support-trained classification corrections combine parent probabilities with source responses and source–target label relationships. A source-activity model adjusts the expected DE count, and up-dated probabilities rank the genes. Measurements and revised predictions enter successive states. Section G gives the transfer, correction, and count-model settings. 

4 Experiments 

4.1 Tasks, data, and evaluation Drug-disjoint prediction. We evaluate 339 held-out SciPlex3 conditions in A549, K562, and MCF7 [Srivatsan et al., 2020], pooling 24-hour responses across doses. Continuous scores cover 2,000 genes; classification retains all reference DE positives and randomly samples up to 200 neg-atives with unadjusted p > 0.1 per condition (Section A). We use the DeepSeek-V4-Flash API for evidence compilation and frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B embeddings [Zhang et al., 2025]. 

Comparators and metrics. We compare BioLORD, chemCPA, CMonge, PRnet, Squidiff [He et al., 2026], PerturbNet [Yu et al., 2025], STATE [Adduri et al., 2025], VCWorld, and Random. The first four use validation-fitted DE adapters and validation-selected decision rules. Squidiff, PerturbNet, and STATE generate cell populations, from which differential testing against matched DMSO yields DE and direction predictions (Section C). VCWorld uses DeepSeek-V4-Flash. Continuous-response metrics include Top50 mean-expression R 2, logFC Pearson, and ∆-Pearson for changes relative to DMSO; within-condition scores are averaged across test conditions. Top50/Top100 panels follow absolute reference Wilcoxon ranks. DE and DIR use AUPRC, AU-ROC, Macro-F1, accuracy, precision, and recall. DIR conditions on reference-DE queries with up-regulation as positive. Joint unchanged/up/down evaluation includes detection and direction errors (Section D). 

Source-grounded explanation. The benchmark contains 966 questions across 72 conditions, with answerability determined by molecular source records. Correctness requires a sup-ported/contradicted judgment and complete proof, or abstention for insufficient evidence (Sec-tion F). 

Observation-feedback protocol. We simulate experimental feedback by revealing existing Sci-Plex3 measurements under fixed acquisition schedules. Each of three partitions withholds one target cell context per test drug; the other two supply measurements. One observation reveals a drug–cell response at 24 hours across all four doses. CellCanon is evaluated with and without Update un-der identical schedules and budgets. Feedback scores use condition-level response and classification readouts, averaged across partitions. Six response models are fine-tuned on revealed source-cell pop-ulations at 48 observations and one source context per drug. Target conditions remain unobserved (Sections G and H.1).

4.6 Reusing evidence analysis reduces token use 

Evidence analysis is shared across genes within a condition; explanation is invoked separately. Rel-ative to VCWorld’s gene-wise prediction and chain-of-thought explanation on matched drug–cell cohorts, CellCanon reduces language-model token use by 99.77% for Predict across 339 conditions and 98.41% for Explain across 72 conditions. Numerical Update reuses compiled evidence and requires no additional language-model calls. Section I gives token totals and cohort definitions. 

5 Discussion and Conclusion 

CellCanon connects accurate single-cell response prediction, source-grounded explanation, and observation-driven updating in an agentic cellular world model. Shared evidence supports gene-specific readouts and generation with substantially lower language-model token use. The world state links predictions to the evidence and observations needed to check and revise them. In simu-lated experimental feedback, measured responses improve predictions for unobserved cell contexts of the same drug.

C External predictor configurations 

The BioLORD, chemCPA, CMonge, and PRnet experiments use SciPlex3 with a 56-drug training split and seed 42. Squidiff, PerturbNet, and STATE use the 54-drug support split, 19 validation drugs, the fixed 2,000-gene panel, and seed 42. The BioLORD, chemCPA, CMonge, and PRnet models provide continuous response estimates. A validation-fitted logistic adapter using absolute predicted log fold change supplies their DE scores. For hard DE decisions, we enumerate validation probability thresholds and select the threshold with the highest Macro-F1 on the sampled validation queries, keeping expression predictions and adapter coefficients fixed. Selected thresholds are 0.038410 for BioLORD, 0.028758 for chemCPA, 0.041701 for CMonge, and 0.047620 for PRnet. BioLORD and CMonge use fitted direction scores. CMonge training and inference. The CMonge adaptation uses a two-layer 512-unit autoencoder with a 100-dimensional latent space. We train it for 100 complete epochs (71,600 updates), se-lecting the checkpoint by validation reconstruction MSE. A conditional transport map uses RDKit descriptors, log dose, and cell-line indicators and trains for 500,000 updates. Checkpoint selec-tion every 10,000 updates uses mean-expression R 2 over 228 validation drug–cell–dose conditions, 15 with negative condition scores floored at zero for this selection criterion. The selected map is the 10,000-update checkpoint. At inference, matched DMSO cells are encoded, transported, decoded, and clipped to nonnegative expression; aggregation produces the pooled condition response. The test output contains 341,202 cells by 2,000 genes. All reported test R 2 values retain negative scores. On the sampled endpoint, CMonge’s DE precision/recall are 0.3432/0.7334 and accuracy is 0.7402; conditional DIR accuracy is 0.6700.

PerturbNet training and inference. PerturbNet [Yu et al., 2025] uses the pretrained 196-dimensional ChemicalVAE representation, a 10-dimensional scVI cell latent, and a conditional in-vertible network with 20 coupling blocks and hidden width 1,024. Molecular features, cell-line indicators, and log dose condition the flow. We train scVI for 700 epochs and the flow for 100 epochs, both with batch size 128; validation ELBO and population-expression MSE select epochs 695 and 80, respectively. Decoded counts use paired panel and full-library sizes sampled from train-ing cells matched by cell line and dose, then transform to log1p CP10K. Molecular canonicalization and equivalent SMILES enumeration supply the chemical encoder inputs.

F.2 Paths and uncertain signs 

A route is eligible when its endpoints, source records, and sign relationships satisfy the path criteria. Known edge signs are composed directly. Unknown signs are assigned jointly across paths so that a shared edge has one value within each completion. These alternative assignments distinguish conclusions that hold for every permitted sign from those that depend on an unresolved interaction. The language model selects and ranks candidate paths. If eligibility review leads to a correction, the included paths are ordered by length and source identifier; otherwise the model’s ordering is retained. Among the 72 evaluation conditions, 63 contain eligible reference paths. On these 63 conditions, valid path@3, precision@3, and recall@3 are each 1.000. Proposition scoring includes all 72 conditions, including the nine with no eligible reference path.

G.3 Selected numerical update settings 

Continuous transfer in Equation (12) uses η = 0 .5, ridge coefficient λ = 1 , and transfer strength 1. The transfer map is fitted on source and target responses from training drugs. At inference, the measured response supplies the query vector for updating the same drug in its hidden cell line. For DE, the conditional-label likelihood updater uses smoothing 200 and strength 0.5. It is mixed equally with a cross-fitted residual update of strength 0.25. A response-feature tree with at most seven leaves contributes an additional correction of strength 0.1. For DIR, the likelihood contribution has selected strength zero; the residual contribution has strength 1 and mixture weight 0.25, with no additional response-feature tree correction. These settings are fixed across the reported budgets. The response-count model standardizes support features and fits a ridge regressor with coefficient 10. Its source-activity count estimate contributes weight 0.25, and 1.25 times the parent’s selected DE count contributes weight 0.75. The updated probabilities determine the ranked gene set. Conse-quently, Update includes both response-conditioned probability changes and readout recalibration. The no-observation path returns the parent’s original outputs, including its hard gene selections. H.1 Adaptation to newly observed responses 

This task asks whether observing a drug response in one cell context improves prediction in an unobserved context. Each partition assigns a hidden cell context to each drug, and measurements come from its other contexts. A measurement reveals the source population at 24 hours, including 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000 nM doses. We evaluate no observations, 48 observed conditions, and one source context per drug. Source selections are fixed before adaptation; target responses are used for evaluation. Scores average the three partitions, pairing each method’s adapted and unadapted predictions on the same hidden targets.
