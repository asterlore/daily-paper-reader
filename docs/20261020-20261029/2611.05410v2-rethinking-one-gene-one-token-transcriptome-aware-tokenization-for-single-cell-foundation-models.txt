ABSTRACT
Single-cell foundation models typically treat each gene as a token, which is both
costly and wasteful. A cell can express thousands of genes, so one token per gene
makes self-attention expensive, yet many of these genes are detected at low levels
and carry too little signal to deserve a token of their own. Genes that are expressed
together could instead share a token, leaving separate tokens for genes that stand
out. We introduce scTAT (Transcriptome-Aware Tokenization), which pairs a
gene-set vocabulary with a per-cell tokenizer. The vocabulary holds overlapping
gene sets that recur with stable relative expression across the pretraining corpus.
Using these sets raises two difficulties. First, which genes should share a token de-
pends on the cell, whereas existing multi-gene tokens fix the grouping in advance.
Second, gene sets of different sizes must be compared with single genes on a com-
mon scale. The tokenizer therefore decides per cell, letting gene sets and single
genes compete for token positions under an expression score normalized by the
square root of set size. Training still reconstructs individual genes, so predictions
stay at gene resolution. In frozen-embedding cell-type annotation, scTAT matches
the strongest one-gene-per-token model with half the token budget. Its advantage
over one-gene-per-token models at the same budget grows as the budget shrinks
and the tokenizer assigns more positions to gene sets. Encoding the same genes
with gene-set tokens also nearly halves the Transformer’s matrix-multiplication
cost. These results show that a token need not hold exactly one gene: when the
tokenizer decides per cell which genes share a token, a fixed budget covers more
of each transcriptome at no cost in annotation accuracy.

However, these two roles pull in opposite directions when each token holds one gene. A cell ex-
presses thousands of genes, so showing the model more of them means a longer input, yet the cost
of self-attention grows quadratically with input length (Vaswani et al., 2017). Models that give each
gene its own token, such as Geneformer and scGPT (Theodoris et al., 2023; Cui et al., 2024), there-
fore keep only a subset of each cell’s genes. Many of the remaining genes are detected at low levels
and carry little signal on their own, yet each still occupies a full position. Expression-weighted sam-
pling favors highly expressed genes when drawing this subset (Qi et al., 2025; Xiong et al., 2026),
but with one gene per position, the token budget still caps how many genes the model sees.
A direct way to lift this cap is to let one token carry several genes, so the same budget reaches more of
each transcriptome. Several single-cell models take this route, grouping genes by curated pathways
or regulons in TOSICA (Chen et al., 2023), learned gene-module projections in Stack (Dong et al.,
2026), or protein-informed soft assignment in scREBOUND (Zhang et al., 2026). Each grouping
is fixed before any cell is seen, which causes two problems. First, relationships the grouping does
not encode are lost at the input: genes outside curated pathways never enter a pathway token, and
co-varying genes in different groups never share a token. Second, which genes belong together
depends on the cell’s type and state. Gene–gene co-expression differs across cell types (Su et al.,
2023): cell-cycle genes rise together in dividing cells (Tirosh et al., 2016), and interferon-stimulated
genes during viral infection (Schneider et al., 2014). Conversely, some identities rest on a single
highly expressed gene, such as insulin in beta cells. A fixed grouping spends positions on programs
a cell does not run and blurs such markers.
To address these two problems, we introduce scTAT (Transcriptome-Aware Tokenization), which
pairs a corpus-mined vocabulary of overlapping gene sets with a per-cell tokenizer. The vocab-
ulary is built along a gene-association graph, which keeps the combinatorial search tractable. It
admits sets whose members co-occur often and keep stable relative levels, using a compression-
based score inspired by the minimum description length (MDL) principle (Rissanen, 1978; Vreeken
et al., 2011). The tokenizer lets gene sets and single genes compete for each cell’s positions under
a size-normalized expression score that balances coverage against per-gene signal. Reconstruction
targets remain individual genes, so predictions stay at gene resolution.
The vocabulary and the tokenizer address these two problems in turn. Mined from co-occurrence
in the pretraining corpus, the vocabulary can group co-varying genes whether or not they share a
curated pathway, and because its sets overlap, no gene is locked into a single group. The tokenizer
selects a gene set only when its members are jointly expressed in the cell, while a gene that dominates
its set, such as insulin in a beta cell, keeps a token of its own; as positions become scarce, it assigns
more of them to gene sets, which lifts the budget cap. In frozen-embedding annotation on ten
datasets, the advantage of scTAT over one-gene-per-token models trained on the same corpus grows
as the budget shrinks, its gene sets are enriched for known pathways and physical associations, and
its frozen encoder remains competitive on gene-level tasks.
Our main contributions are as follows:
• We identify the token as a bottleneck: one gene per token caps how many genes a model
sees, while existing multi-gene tokens fix their grouping in advance, losing relationships
outside their groups and ignoring cell-dependent co-expression.
• We construct a gene-set vocabulary and design a per-cell tokenizer: graph-constrained
search and an MDL-inspired score admit overlapping gene sets that recur with stable rela-
tive expression, and the tokenizer lets these sets compete with single genes for each cell’s
token positions, so which genes share a token follows each cell’s expression.
• We conduct a comprehensive evaluation on ten cell-type annotation datasets and two gene-
level tasks, with efficiency, ablation, and pathway analyses. scTAT matches the strongest
one-gene-per-token model with half the token budget and nearly halves the Transformer
FLOPs needed to encode the same genes.

RELATED WORK
What a token holds in single-cell Transformers.
Most single-cell foundation models give each
token one gene. The token may combine gene identity with an expression value, as in scBERT (Yang
et al., 2022) and scGPT (Cui et al., 2024), or encode expression through the gene’s rank position,

as in Geneformer (Theodoris et al., 2023) and tGPT (Shen et al., 2023); scMulan adds metadata
and task tokens (Bian et al., 2024). Biological knowledge typically enriches the gene embedding
itself: GeneCompass injects regulatory and functional priors (Yang et al., 2024), and UCE uses
protein-language-model embeddings (Rosen et al., 2026). scTAT instead lets a gene-association
graph restrict which genes may share a token and corpus statistics decide which sets are admitted.
Tokenizers in biological foundation models.
Genomic models vary in their input representations:
DNABERT uses fixed k-mers and DNABERT-2 learns byte-pair-encoding merges (Ji et al., 2021;
Zhou et al., 2023). MutBERT represents each nucleotide position as a probability distribution de-
rived from population allele frequencies (Long et al., 2025). Expression profiles have no gene order,
so single-cell models that group genes into tokens take membership from other sources. TOSICA
uses curated pathways or regulons, masking a trainable projection so that each token reads a speci-
fied gene set (Chen et al., 2023); Stack learns gene-module projections jointly with an encoder that
attends across genes and cells (Dong et al., 2026); scREBOUND learns a protein-informed soft as-
signment that pools genes into meta-genes (Zhang et al., 2026). scConcept samples disjoint gene
panels to form two views of each cell and learns cell representations through contrastive pretrain-
ing (Bahrami et al., 2025). Whereas TOSICA, Stack, and scREBOUND fix their grouping before
any cell is seen, scTAT selects per cell which gene sets and single genes enter the input. Its gene
sets are mined from the training corpus with a candidate-local variant of compression-based itemset
mining (Vreeken et al., 2011) that adds expression-consistency and graph terms.
Efficient single-cell encoders.
Other work reduces cost elsewhere in the model. Performer ap-
proximates softmax attention (Choromanski et al., 2021); xTrimoGene exploits expression spar-
sity with an asymmetric encoder–decoder (Gong et al., 2023); scBenchmark evaluates expression-
weighted sampling and alternative encoders (Qi et al., 2025); CellPLM treats whole cells as tokens
and attends across cells (Wen et al., 2024). In vision Transformers, token merging and pruning
shorten the sequence inside the network by combining similar tokens or dropping uninformative
ones (Bolya et al., 2023; Rao et al., 2021). scTAT instead decides before encoding which genes may
share a token, from corpus statistics, so every token keeps an explicit gene membership. Shortening
the gene sequence this way is complementary to efficient attention and to gene sampling.

METHOD
3.1
OVERVIEW
scTAT builds a gene-set vocabulary offline (Section 3.2), selects gene sets and single genes for each
cell (Section 3.3), and trains a Transformer to reconstruct masked genes (Section 3.4; Figure 2).
Let G be the gene universe and x ∈R|G|
≥0 a cell’s processed expression profile; a zero means the
gene was not detected. The active genes are A(x) = {g : xg > 0}. The vocabulary V contains
two kinds of entries, each an explicit set of gene identities: gene sets (multi-gene entries) and single
genes (singletons, one per gene in G). Entries may overlap. An entry is shared across cells, whereas
a token is the entry’s instance in one cell, carrying that cell’s expression. For each cell, tokenization
selects at most K entries T (x) ⊆V and adds a CLS token for the cell readout; when K is small,
some active genes remain unrepresented.
3.2
VOCABULARY CONSTRUCTION
Proposing candidates on a gene graph.
The number of possible gene sets grows combinatorially,
so we search only within neighborhoods of a gene graph. We build a mutual k-nearest-neighbor
graph from the normalized gene embeddings of pretrained scGPT: two genes are connected when
each ranks among the other’s top-k neighbors by cosine similarity, and the similarity is the edge
weight. Its edge weights also enter the coherence term of the admission score below; only the graph
comes from scGPT, and no scGPT weights initialize scTAT.
Admitting gene sets by compression.
A gene set deserves a token when it recurs across many
cells and its members keep similar relative expression, since one token then summarizes them with
little loss. We measure both properties on a discovery sample of 50,000 training cells, split into

Searching for gene sets.
Starting from each gene as an anchor a, we greedily extend a candidate
with genes from the anchor’s neighbor set N(a) while the admission score improves and the support
constraint holds, so every candidate lies in {a}∪N(a) (Algorithm 1). Adding all single genes to the
admitted sets completes the vocabulary V, which contains 79,536 gene sets of 2–32 genes (median
4) and 60,694 single genes (Appendix Figure 5).

4.2
RESULTS AND ANALYSIS
Q1. How does annotation accuracy change with the token budget?
Tables 1 and 2 compare
frozen embeddings from scTAT at 64 to 512 tokens with four pretrained baselines, each given 512
genes by uniform or expression-weighted sampling. With 256 tokens, half the baselines’ budget,
scTAT matches the strongest baseline, expression-weighted scBenchmark (79.92% versus 79.83%
KNN and 81.21% versus 81.07% linear-probe accuracy), and with 128 or 64 tokens it remains
above every uniformly sampled 512-token baseline. Against models pretrained with one gene per
token at the same budget (Figure 3a,b), its margin widens from 0.3 to 8.0 KNN points and from
0.4 to 6.4 linear-probe points as the budget falls from 512 to 64 tokens, so gene-set tokens matter
most when positions are scarce. At 512 tokens, scTAT is also the highest under both probes, 0.29
and 0.38 points above expression-weighted scBenchmark (paired over five splits: 0.29 ± 0.14 and
0.38 ± 0.20; Appendix B).
Q2. How does token granularity change with the token budget?
Because gene sets and single
genes compete for positions, the tokenizer’s granularity is an outcome rather than a fixed design
(Figure 3c). On validation cells, gene sets fill 74.9%, 66.2%, 54.4%, and 41.3% of the selected
positions at 64, 128, 256, and 512 tokens, each token covers 5.2 to 2.1 genes on average, and the
selected tokens reach 20% to 65% of a cell’s expressed genes. At 64 tokens, half of the positions
hold sets of at least five genes, against 15% at 512 tokens. About two-thirds of the first 128 positions

Q3. How much encoder computation do gene-set tokens save?
If a token carries several genes,
fewer tokens should suffice to represent a given set of genes. We test this with the main models at
64, 128, 256, and 512 tokens: each cell’s selected gene union is expanded into single-gene inputs
for a fixed scBenchmark checkpoint, and both encoders process the same 10,000 cells (Table 3).
At 512 tokens, encoding the selected genes as gene-set tokens rather than single-gene tokens lowers
Transformer matrix-multiplication cost by 48.5% and peak GPU memory by 63.8%; at 64 tokens, it
lowers them by 75.5% and 53.9%, and GPU encoding time by 34.5%. The FLOPs savings grow as
the budget shrinks because each token then covers more genes (Figure 3c). Fewer tokens also mean
less computation for the same accuracy: at full budget, scTAT at 256 tokens matches one gene per
token at 512 tokens (Figure 3a,b) with 40% of the GFLOPs per cell (1.6 versus 4.0), whereas scGPT
and Geneformer need 26 and 97 GFLOPs per cell at 512 tokens.

BIOLOGICAL SUPPORT FOR THE VOCABULARY
External biological support.
The admitted gene sets are enriched for known pathways and phys-
ical associations. On a prespecified panel of 1,000 gene sets, 20.10% have a significant Reactome
pathway hit (Milacic et al., 2024), against 3.87–4.83% for stratified gene-label permutations; Bi-
oGRID physical associations (Oughtred et al., 2021) show the same pattern. Random sets from
the same graph neighborhoods reach 17.05%, and corpus-based admission adds a further 1.18-fold
for pathways and 1.38-fold for physical associations (Appendix A). Figure 4 shows six frequently

endosomal set containing the HLA-E/B2M complex. These sets group co-regulated or co-assembled
genes: ribosomal proteins, mitochondrial transcripts processed by FASTK-family proteins (Ohkubo
et al., 2021), and MHC class I chains with B2M. Appendix A describes each set.
5
CONCLUSION
scTAT replaces the one-gene token with a choice made for each cell. A vocabulary mined from
the pretraining corpus supplies overlapping gene sets whose members co-occur with stable relative
expression, and a size-normalized expression score lets these sets compete with single genes for ev-
ery position, while reconstruction targets remain individual genes. With frozen embeddings, scTAT
matches the strongest one-gene-per-token model with half the tokens and exceeds one-gene-per-
token models at every budget from 64 to 512 tokens, by the widest margin when tokens are scarce
and the tokenizer assigns most positions to gene sets. It encodes the same genes with nearly half
the Transformer FLOPs and less GPU memory, its gene sets are enriched for known pathways and
physical associations, and its frozen encoder remains competitive on gene-level tasks.
These results show that what a token holds is a design choice rather than a fixed convention. De-
ciding per cell which genes share a token lets a fixed budget cover more of each transcriptome, with
the largest benefit under tight budgets, where one gene per token must leave most of each transcrip-
tome out. Because it shortens the input before encoding, this choice is complementary to efficient
attention and to gene sampling. Natural next steps are combinatorial perturbation prediction as in
GEARS (Roohani et al., 2024), refining the vocabulary jointly with encoder training, and vocabu-
laries for other species and modalities, such as chromatin accessibility or spatial transcriptomics.

Null-control construction and testing.
To reduce redundant entries in the null comparison, we
greedily prune entries in descending MDL-score order using a Jaccard-overlap criterion, retaining
55,137 representatives, and uniformly sample a fixed panel of 1,000. The two prespecified endpoints
are the fraction of entries with a within-entry Bonferroni-adjusted Reactome hit at the prespecified
significance level and the mean multi-publication association density. Three controls globally per-
mute gene labels within strata, preserving entry sizes and all overlaps. The first conditions on detec-
tion frequency and annotation availability; the others additionally condition on prior-graph degree,
then experimental-network degree. A fourth control draws random stars from the prior graph with
the same inferred anchor and size as each entry. The inferred anchor is a deterministically chosen
member adjacent to all other members.
Each control uses repeated random draws. One-sided Monte Carlo P values use the plus-one cor-
rection; Bonferroni adjustment covers the eight primary comparisons (four controls, two endpoints).
For the fixed panel of 1,000 entries sampled after overlap pruning, the Reactome hit rate is 20.10%
and mean density of physical associations supported by at least two publications is 2.460%. Here,
a pathway hit uses within-entry correction, whereas the vocabulary-wide counts above additionally
control across entries. The three stratified permutation controls yield null means of 3.87–4.83% for

GENE-NETWORK EVALUATION DETAILS
Input and scoring.
Following the Han hESC workflow of scPRINT (Kalfon et al., 2025), all mod-
els start from the same panel: the first 1,024 Han hESC cells and 5,000 highly variable genes. Each
model keeps its native tokenizer, preprocessing, and vocabulary filtering, which leaves 4,984–5,000
scorable genes. BenGRN 1.2.4 scores all candidate edges against the Full, ChIP, and KO references
(22, 41, and 112–113 transcription factors), and we report trapezoidal AUPRC without a held-out
edge split or label-based head selection. The random baseline is the positive-edge prevalence of
each reference, which varies by less than 0.001 across models; Table 5 reports its mean.
Models and extraction.
GENIE3 (Huynh-Thu et al., 2010) is fitted on the expression panel; all
pretrained encoders are frozen. scGPT (Cui et al., 2024) uses its whole-human checkpoint and the
reference workflow’s last-layer, eight-head mean. Geneformer (Theodoris et al., 2023) uses the
12L–30M checkpoint with its native ranked input and the mean over all layers and heads. scPRINT
uses the arithmetic mean of its 128 heads. scTAT (the main 512-token model) averages its 48 heads
and pools gene-level query/key representations from its native input, with zero scores for genes it
does not cover. Query–key scores are transposed once to the scorer’s source–target orientation.

PERTURBATION EVALUATION DETAILS
Shared data and splits.
We follow the Norman_1 workflow in PertEval-scFM (Wenteler et al.,
2025), starting from its original Norman raw-data source. After eligibility filtering, the task contains
11,855 control cells, 56,147 treated cells, 101 perturbation conditions, and 2,060 target genes. Eligi-
bility requires usable controls expressing the perturbed gene. One condition without such controls is
excluded under the upstream rule. All methods in Table 5(b) use the same 101 retained conditions,
2,060 target genes, test cells, saved SPECTRA splits, and control-pooling draws. Gene identities
are resolved without summing duplicate-symbol counts; encoder-input name mapping preserves the
original target columns and perturbation labels. We use the official split generator.

Control-derived features.
For pretrained models, basal features average embeddings over shared
draws of control cells. A condition feature averages embeddings of eligible controls after removing
or nullifying the perturbed gene. The predictor receives the concatenated basal and condition fea-
tures. Observed treated expression supplies supervised targets and SPECTRA split construction, but
never encoder features.
scTAT uses frozen, unnormalized CLS embeddings from the main 512-token model, which also to-
kenizes its inputs. Its benchmark adapter normalizes and log-transforms control expression before
nullifying the target gene. Geneformer uses the six-layer, 256-dimensional 6L–30M checkpoint
specified by PertEval-scFM, whereas annotation uses its V2 checkpoint. It tokenizes raw controls
by median-normalized rank, removes the target gene, and averages the non-padding final-layer em-
beddings. The scFoundation model (Hao et al., 2024) uses its native single-cell adapter with the
target count zeroed before normalization, and concatenates its two special-token readouts with max-
imum and mean gene embeddings. scGPT uses the whole-human checkpoint, native positive-gene
binning and sampling, and per-cell L2-normalized CLS embeddings before control pooling. No
additional L2 normalization is applied after pooling. Each model thus uses its own adapter and
readout.
The raw-expression MLP uses control-derived expression pseudo-bulks and condition features given
by the perturbed gene’s Pearson correlations with target genes, following the upstream reference.
“Raw-expression” here denotes the absence of a pretrained encoder, not unprocessed sequencing
counts. The Mean baseline preserves the upstream batch-dependent rule: it predicts each test batch’s
mean basal expression minus the corresponding basal vector, with no learned predictor.
Targets, fitting, and scoring.
Normalization, target-gene selection, and SPECTRA split construc-
tion follow the upstream transductive preparation using all retained data. Targets measure expression
changes in normalized/log space, retaining the upstream subset-local basal subtraction. Test condi-
tions are held out from predictor fitting, while validation cells are drawn from training conditions.
MLPs share the predictor recipe, with widths adapted to the representation dimension, and are se-
lected by validation MSE before one final test evaluation.
Test MSE weights all retained cell–gene elements equally. Table 5(b) reports means and sample
standard deviations across three paired split/predictor repetitions with fixed encoder weights. Each
integrated score is computed within a repetition by trapezoidal integration over the stated s interval
and is not normalized by interval length.
