ABSTRACT
Full fine-tuning has achieved remarkable success on reasoning-intensive tasks,
but it often requires substantial GPU resource consumption. Parameter-efficient
fine-tuning (PEFT) provides a promising alternative for reducing this cost, yet
we observe a substantial performance gap on reasoning-intensive tasks. In this
paper, we present three key observations showing that this gap is closely associ-
ated with norm-related under-adaptation, where PEFT produces markedly smaller
weight-update norms than full fine-tuning. Based on these observations, we pro-
pose Norm-Guided Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (NormTune), a simple yet ef-
fective strategy that adaptively strengthens insufficiently updated layers according
to their weight-update norms. Extensive experiments show that NormTune con-
sistently enhances multiple PEFT methods across challenging reasoning bench-
marks. Specifically, when trained on S1K-1.1, integrating NormTune into rank-8
LoRA, Bone, and MoRA yields substantial average accuracy improvements of
24.24%, 11.33%, and 16.21% , respectively, across AIME 2024, MATH 500,
GPQA, and AIME 2025. Moreover, NormTune-enhanced rank-256 LoRA, Bone,
and MoRA achieve performance comparable to S1 full fine-tuning, with respec-
tive average gains of 2.17%, 0.93%, and 3.00% over this baseline.

INTRODUCTION
Recent studies (Wu et al., 2025; OpenAI, 2024; Guo et al., 2025a; Yu et al., 2025a; Xie et al.,
2024) find that, for challenging reasoning tasks, carefully increasing inference-time compute can
significantly boost performance. However, most of these approaches depend on substantial compute
budgets, specialized training pipelines, or complex inference-time search, making them expensive,
hard to reproduce, and impractical for broad adoption. To address these limitations, many stud-
ies(Muennighoff et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025b) have explored
simpler and more reproducible alternatives based on supervised fine-tuning (SFT). These methods,
however, still depend on full fine-tuning, which becomes progressively less practical as model scale
increases, particularly in compute-constrained settings. For example, full fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-
32B-Instruct (Team, 2024) on a dataset with a 32K context length requires 562.07 GB of memory
even with gradient checkpointing, making it difficult to apply under limited compute resources. To
address this challenge, we consider parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods (Hu et al., 2021;
Lin et al., 2024a; Feng et al., 2025), which adapt large pretrained models to downstream tasks by
updating only a small subset of parameters while keeping the backbone model frozen.
Motivated by this efficiency, we apply PEFT to reasoning-intensive adaptation, while noting that
its reduced training cost may come with non-negligible performance risks in such challenging set-
tings. Thus we investigate the question: Can low-rank PEFT retain the Full-FT comparable
adaptation capability? As shown on the right side of Figure 1a, existing PEFT methods exhibit

gap, we present three key observations. Observation 1: PEFT methods exhibit a much larger
loss contribution on reasoning tokens such as transition, self-verification, template, and reasoning-
boundary tokens compared with Full-FT at earlier training. During training, PEFT methods exhibit
a smaller relative reduction in loss on normal tokens (i.e., all tokens other than reasoning tokens)
than on reasoning tokens, whereas Full-FT exhibits the opposite pattern, as shown in Figure 1b.
Observation 2: The gradients induced by reasoning token and normal token losses exhibit substan-
tial conflicts across most trainable parameters in earlier layers. However, the total token gradients
remain aligned with the normal token optimization direction, while its effective magnitude is par-
tially reduced by the conflicting reasoning token gradients. This gradient interference weakens the
effective parameter updates in earlier layers. Observation 3: PEFT methods induce substantially
smaller weight-update norms than full fine-tuning, especially in earlier layers. To achieve perfor-
mance comparable to Full-FT, we expect PEFT methods to exhibit similar layer-wise weight-update
magnitudes as Full-FT. Based on this observation, we propose Norm-Guided Parameter-Efficient
Fine-Tuning (NormTune), which reduces the gradients conflicts in earlier layers, reallocates opti-
mization strength toward under-adapted layers and improves model performance.
Our contributions can be summarized as follows:
• We systematically investigate the limitations of PEFT on reasoning-intensive tasks and
present three key observation, revealing that PEFT induces substantially smaller fine-
tuning-induced weight changes, which is closely associated with its performance degra-
dation.
• Motivated by these observations, we propose a novel PEFT method named NormTune,
which reduces the gradients conflicts in earlier layers and reallocates optimization strength
toward under-adapted layers. To the best of our knowledge, NormTune is the first approach
that enables PEFT methods to achieve comparable performance relative to full fine-tuning
on reasoning-intensive tasks.
• Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of NormTune. Specif-
ically, when trained on S1K-1.1, NormTune improves rank-8 LoRA, Bone, and MoRA by
24.24%, 11.33%, and 16.21% in average accuracy across AIME 2024, MATH 500, GPQA,
and AIME 2025, respectively. Moreover, NormTune-enhanced rank-256 LoRA, Bone, and
MoRA achieve performance comparable to S1 full fine-tuning, with respective average
gains of 2.17%, 0.93%, and 3.00% over this baseline.

METHODOLOGY
2.1
KEY OBSERVATIONS ON REASONING-INTENSIVE TASKS
Observation 1: PEFT methods exhibit a much larger loss contribution on reasoning tokens
compared with Full-FT at earlier training. To investigate the accuracy gap between PEFT and
Full-FT on reasoning-intensive tasks, we analyze how training loss is distributed across tokens and
how it evolves during fine-tuning, motivated by prior work Lin et al. (2024b); Pang et al. (2025).
Specifically, we sample 16 sequences from S1K-1.1 (Muennighoff et al., 2025) and evaluate token-
level cross-entropy losses on the same sequences at training step 30, immediately after warm-up,
and step 310, at the end of training, using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. We inspect the high-loss tail using
the empirical 99th percentile (P99)Sato et al. (2022) of token-level losses as a cutoff. Most tokens
above this threshold correspond to transitions, self-verification expressions, templates, or reasoning
boundaries such as “Alternatively” and “Wait”.. For this analysis, we refer to tokens serving these
four roles as reasoning tokens, and to all remaining tokens as normal tokens. As shown in Figure 1b,
PEFT methods exhibit a much larger loss contribution on reasoning tokens compared with Full-FT
at earlier training. After training at step 310 , PEFT methods exhibit a smaller relative reduction in
loss on normal tokens than on reasoning tokens, whereas Full-FT exhibits the opposite pattern. This
indicates that PEFT disproportionately allocates its optimization capacity toward adapting reasoning
token patterns, while providing insufficient adaptation for normal token representations compared
with Full-FT.

substantial conflicts across most trainable parameters in earlier layers.

2.2
NORMTUNE
Overall, the three observations suggest that improving PEFT on reasoning-intensive tasks requires
stronger adaptation for normal tokens, particularly in earlier layers. Observation 1 reveals an im-
balance in token-level loss reduction, suggesting insufficient adaptation for normal tokens relative
to Full-FT. Observation 2 provides a explanation: although reasoning-token and normal-token gra-
dients conflict in earlier layers, the total gradient remains positively aligned with the normal-token
gradient. This interference therefore attenuates the gradient component along the normal-token di-
rection while preserving its positive alignment. This pattern is consistent with Observation 3, which
shows substantially smaller weight-update norms in these layers under PEFT. These findings mo-
tivate increasing the learning rates of under-adapted layers to compensate for attenuated updates.
Specially, for fixed parameters, gradients, and optimizer state, increasing a layer’s learning rate
scales the magnitude of its parameter update without changing its direction.
Motivated by these observations, we propose Norm-Guided Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
(NormTune), which assigns larger learning rates to weakly updated layers based on their weight-
update norms, thereby improving the adaptation of under-trained layers. In general, U Full

EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
Datasets.
For training, we use the S1K-1.1 dataset Muennighoff et al. (2025), the Z1-Code-
Reasoning107K dataset Yu et al. (2025b), the Data-repetition dataset Kopiczko et al. (2026), the
GovReport dataset for long-context understandingHuang et al. (2021), and the Commonsense Rea-
soning dataset for short reasoning. We further evaluate different models on several challenging
tasks. The reasoning-intensive tasks consist of AIME 2024 Art of Problem Solving (2024), MATH
500 Hendrycks et al. (2021), GPQA Rein et al. (2024), and AIME 2025 Art of Problem Solving
(2025). The code-scientific reasoning tasks consist of MATH 500, GPQA, LiveCodeBench Jain
et al. (2024), and BigCodeBench-Hard Zhuo et al. (2024).
Compared Methods.
The baseline methods are broadly divided into two categories: reinforce-
ment learning (RL) and supervised fine-tuning (SFT). In the RL category, we compare against Ope-
nAI o1 OpenAI (2024), o3 OpenAI (2025), DeepSeek-R1 Guo et al. (2025b), and QwQ-32B Qwen
Team (2025). In the SFT category, we compare against S1(Full-FT) Muennighoff et al. (2025), Z1-
7B(Full-FT) Yu et al. (2025b), Qwen3-4B(Full-FT) Kopiczko et al. (2026) and OLMo-3-7B(Full-
FT) Kopiczko et al. (2026). In addition, we compare against several representative PEFT methods,
including DoRA Liu et al. (2024), AdaLoRA Zhang et al. (2023), LoRA+ Hayou et al. (2024),
rsLoRA Kalajdzievski (2023), LoRA Hu et al. (2021), BoneKang (2024) and MoRAJiang et al.
(2024). We further incorporate TempBalance Zhou et al. (2023) into the PEFT baselines for com-
parison.
Evaluation Metrics.
For the S1K-1.1 and Z1-Code-Reasoning107K settings, we evaluate each
locally evaluated checkpoint using greedy decoding with temperature T = 0, generating one com-
pletion per problem in each evaluation run. We repeat evaluation on the same checkpoint and the
same benchmark questions for R = 4 runs on MATH 500 and GPQA, and R = 16 runs on AIME
2024, AIME 2025, LiveCodeBench, and BigCodeBench-Hard, where applicable. Tables 1 and 2
report the mean accuracy across these evaluation runs. These repetitions are evaluation runs of a
fixed checkpoint, rather than independent training runs or stochastic decoding samples. The Avg.
column is the unweighted mean of the four benchmark accuracies. For Qwen3-4B and OLMo-3-7B,
we retain the sampling-based Avg@k and Pass@k metrics under the decoding configurations speci-
fied in Table 22. Results taken from prior publications retain their original evaluation protocols and
are explicitly distinguished from locally evaluated results. Details of the uncertainty estimation are
provided in Table 22. For the environment and settings also provided in Appendix A.3.

Rank Sensitivity.
As shown in Figure 5a, increasing the rank generally improves both LoRA and
NormTune+LoRA, while NormTune+LoRA consistently outperforms LoRA across all ranks. The
gains are particularly pronounced in low-rank regimes: at rank 8, NormTune+LoRA improves the
average score from 37.40% to 61.64%. NormTune+LoRA achieves its best average performance at
rank 256, reaching 68.6% and slightly exceeding the Full-FT baseline of 66.43%. Further increasing
the rank to 512 reduces the score to 65.17%, indicating that larger ranks do not necessarily yield
better reasoning-intensive performance. These results show that NormTune effectively improves
LoRA across different rank budgets, especially when the adaptation capacity is highly constrained.

3.3
HYPERPARAMETER SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
We analyze the sensitivity of NormTune to the bucket number n, which determines the granularity
of layer-wise learning-rate allocation. As shown in Figure 5b, NormTune is robust to a wide range
of n. Across n ∈{4, 8, 16, 32, 64}, the GPQA accuracy only changes from 62.12% to 64.14%,
showing limited sensitivity to this hyperparameter. The best result is obtained at n = 16, where
NormTune achieves 64.14%. This indicates that a moderate bucket number provides sufficient layer-
wise differentiation without introducing unnecessary fragmentation.

CONCLUSION
In this work, we studied parameter-efficient fine-tuning for reasoning-intensive tasks with test-time
scaling. Through three key observations, we propose Norm-Guided Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
(NormTune), which adaptively enhances under-trained layers and thereby substantially improves
performance on reasoning-intensive tasks. Extensive experiments on three benchmarks demonstrate
that NormTune consistently improves different PEFT methods and can achieve performance com-
parable to full fine-tuning with substantially lower resource consumption.

A.1
RELATED WORK
A.1.1
TEST-TIME SCALING FOR REASONING
Test-time scaling (OpenAI, 2024; Guo et al., 2025a; OpenAI, 2025; Bsharat & Shen, 2025; Xu
et al., 2025; Kopiczko et al., 2026) has recently emerged as an effective way to improve reasoning
performance by allocating more computation at inference time. Existing methods can be broadly
grouped into two categories: reinforcement learning (RL) (OpenAI, 2025; 2024; Guo et al., 2025a)
and supervised fine-tuning (SFT)(Bsharat & Shen, 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Kopiczko et al., 2026).
For reinforcement learning (RL), despite their strong performance, these methods often depend on
expensive post-training pipelines, are challenging to reproduce, or involve additional inference-time
search components.
To address these limitations, recent studies have explored simpler and more reproducible forms of
test-time scaling based on supervised fine-tuning (Xu et al., 2025; An et al., 2025; Kopiczko et al.,
2026). Although these methods are generally easier to reproduce and adopt than reinforcement-
learning-based approaches, training them still requires substantial computational resources, espe-
cially for large models, and often depends on access to high-memory GPUs.
A.1.2
PARAMETER-EFFICIENT FINE-TUNING
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)(Houlsby et al., 2019; Li & Liang, 2021; Liu et al., 2024;
Feng et al., 2025; Yao et al., 2024) is introduced to adapt pretrained models without updating all
backbone parameters, thereby substantially reducing GPU memory consumption. Early PEFT meth-
ods(Houlsby et al., 2019; Li & Liang, 2021) add small task-specific modules or prompts while keep-
ing the main model frozen. However, these methods often introduce additional inference latency
and increase the computational cost of generation. To address this issue, low-rank adaptation(Zhang
et al., 2023; Kalajdzievski, 2023; Hayou et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2024; Jiang et al.,
2024), which can be merged into the backbone model during inference, has become the most widely
used PEFT approach for large language models. Although these methods greatly improve memory
efficiency during fine-tuning, they often underperform full fine-tuning, especially under low-rank
settings. In this paper, we propose Norm-Guided Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (NormTune),
which adaptively enhances under-trained layers and thereby substantially improves performance on
reasoning-intensive tasks.

OTHER EXPERIMENTS
We provide additional results to further support the observations in the main experiments. Table 6
reports the detailed results corresponding to Figure 5a. Across all ranks, NormTune consistently
improves LoRA on reasoning-intensive tasks. The gains are especially pronounced at low ranks.
Notably, when the rank is set to 256, NormTune+LoRA achieves an average accuracy of 68.6%,
slightly surpassing Full-FT, which further demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed method.
Tables 11, 16, and 17 provide the detailed results corresponding to Figure 1a. These results high-
light a clear difference between reasoning-intensive tasks and other task types. On commonsense
reasoning and long-context understanding tasks, PEFT methods with rank 8 already achieve perfor-
mance close to Full-FT. In contrast, the same low-rank PEFT setting performs substantially worse
on reasoning-intensive tasks, as shown in Table 11.
In addition, we further conduct a sensitivity study using 10 (before warm-up), 30, and 50 (after
warm-up) steps with LoRA (rank=256) under the same setting as Table 1. As shown in Table
12, 10 steps lead to noticeably worse performance, while the results remain stable after 30 steps,
supporting our choice. Since the probing steps are included in the total training budget, using 50
steps delays the activation of NormTune by 20 updates compared to 30 steps, which may affect
norm adaptation and lead to a slight performance drop. We also conduct an additional ablation on
Qwen3-4B using NormTune+LoRA(256) on GPQA. Figure 6 show the same trend: performance
is relatively insensitive to n, and n = 16 remains a strong default. This suggests that the optimal
bucket number does not strongly depend on model size or depth in our tested settings.

ENVIRONMENT AND SETTINGS
All experiments are conducted on eight NVIDIA A800 80GB GPUs. Unless otherwise specified,
we follow the original training settings of S1 Muennighoff et al. (2025), Z1 Yu et al. (2025b),
and Data-Repetition Kopiczko et al. (2026) for the main hyperparameters, including the warmup
schedule, the number of training steps, the optimizer, and the use of gradient checkpointing. We
use Qwen3-4B, Qwen-2.5-Coder-7B-InstructYu et al. (2025b), OLMo-3-7BOlmo et al. (2025) and
Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct as the base models for our PEFT experiments across different benchmarks.
Unless otherwise specified, we compute layer-wise norm after training step 30, which is included
in the full training steps, set the number of buckets to n = 16, set base learning rate to the ηmin
of each PEFT method, set the random seed to 42 during training, and use ηmax = 1 × 10−4. In
addition, to ensure a fair comparison, Full-FT and PEFT use identical optimization hyperparameters,
following the configurations adopted in the corresponding papers, which also include experiments
on the GovReport Huang et al. (2021) and commonsense reasoning benchmarks. NormTune does
not require a separate probing run. The first 30 updates are included in the normal training budget.
The additional cost of the whole probing stage is negligible: on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, it requires
only 0.018 GPU-hours on 8 A800 GPUs.
Beyond S1K-1.1 Muennighoff et al. (2025), Z1-Code-Reasoning107K Yu et al. (2025b), and Data-
Repetition Kopiczko et al. (2026), we further fine-tune and evaluate on two additional benchmarks:
commonsense reasoning and long-context understanding. For commonsense reasoning, we con-
sider eight representative tasks, including HellaSwag Zellers et al. (2019), OpenBookQA (OBQA)
Mihaylov et al. (2018), Social IQA (SIQA) Sap et al. (2019), WinoGrande Sakaguchi et al. (2020),
BoolQ Clark et al. (2019), PIQA Bisk et al. (2020), ARC-Challenge (ARC-c), and ARC-Easy (ARC-
e) Clark et al. (2018). For long-context understanding, we use GovReport Huang et al. (2021),
a long-document summarization benchmark consisting of reports from U.S. government agencies
and their corresponding summaries. We randomly sample 1,000 examples from the training split
for fine-tuning and evaluate the resulting models on the official test split. These evaluations en-
able a comprehensive assessment of the effectiveness of our method. The detailed experimental
configurations for full fine-tuning and PEFT methods, including LoRA, Bone, and MoRA, are re-
ported in Tables 19, 20, 21, and 22. We set a = 16, 512 for RsLoRA and exp temp = 10 and
esd update steps = 2 for TempBalance. All experiments are conducted on eight NVIDIA A800
GPUs and completed within 24 hours.
