## ABSTRACT 

Generating accurate 3D molecules requires jointly determining a variable num-ber of atoms, their identities, and geometry across spatial scales: global shape emerges coarsely, whereas chemical bonds demand fine local precision. Existing atom-based diffusion and flow models operate largely at a single scale, while dense spatial fields become costly at high resolution. We introduce OctMole, a multiscale octree flow framework for 3D molecular generation. A hierarchical occupancy flow progressively resolves sparse molecular support from coarse to fine, and a joint atom flow assigns atom types and continuous in-cell offsets to occupied leaves. This decomposition combines efficient global structure with subcell geometric precision, while cell corruption improves robustness to gener-ated support. On QM9 and GEOM-DRUG, OctMole achieves strong chemical, distributional, and geometric quality, with particularly clear gains over Uni-3DAR on GEOM-DRUG and substantial efficiency gains over dense 3D generation. As an additional capability, intermediate occupancy states and selected regions provide spatial conditioning without task-specific retraining, supporting multiscale shape conditioning, regional editing, linker generation, and shape interpolation. Code and pretrained checkpoints are available here and here, respectively. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Generating accurate molecular 3D structures is a geometric modeling problem at a chemically unforgiving scale. A molecule generative model must determine the number of atoms, their chemical identities, and their spatial arrangement. Even small coordinate errors can alter chemical bonds, valencies, and conformational energy. These requirements span distinct spatial scales: global shape and molecular extent emerge at a coarse scale, whereas bond lengths, angles, and local chemical environments demand fine geometric precision. Diffusion and flow models have made substantial progress by jointly generating atomic coordinates and chemical identities (Hoogeboom et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2023; Irwin et al., 2025). Most operate directly on atoms at a single spatial scale, leaving the coarse-to-fine structure of molecular geometry implicit. Dense molecular fields provide an explicit spatial representation, but their cost grows rapidly with resolution (Pinheiro et al., 2023), creating a tension between global coverage and local accuracy. Sparse spatial hierarchies offer a natural alternative because molecules occupy only a small fraction of their surrounding 3D domain. Coarse-to-fine octree sequences and structured 3D latents have demonstrated the effectiveness of hierarchical representations for 3D shape generation (Wei et al., 2025; Xiang et al., 2025). Uni-3DAR, the most directly related molecular generator, extends octree tokenization across several 3D domains (Lu et al., 2025). However, it samples autoregressively and encodes each atom’s in-cell coordinates as a set of 24 discrete tokens. The challenge is therefore to combine efficient multiscale generation with the continuous local precision needed for chemically plausible geometry. We introduce OctMole , a multiscale octree flow framework for 3D molecular generation. It first gen-erates spatial support with a Hierarchical Occupancy Flow , then assigns atom types and continuous coordinates with a Joint Atom Flow . The occupancy flow starts from a single root cell and progres-sively resolves sparse molecular support from coarse to fine, jointly generating all cells at each scale; its occupied leaves determine the number and approximate locations of atoms. Conditioned on these finest occupied cells, the atom flow jointly generates categorical atom types and continuous in-cell 1arXiv:2610.16586v1 [cs.LG] 12 Oct 2026 offsets. The hierarchy captures global molecular structure efficiently, while the continuous offsets recover chemically sensitive local geometry beyond the grid resolution. Optional cell corruption makes the atom flow more robust to errors in generated support. Across QM9 and GEOM-DRUG, OctMole generates chemically plausible molecules with strong distributional fidelity and local geometric accuracy. The gains are particularly clear on the larger and more challenging GEOM-DRUG dataset, where OctMole achieves 86.82% molecular stability and 96.90% connectedness, representing relative improvements of 11.7% and 7.8%, respectively, over Uni-3DAR, while outperforming it on five of the six distributional distances. Moreover, OctMole remains the most stable generator across all atom-count bins. In a system-level comparison, it also provides substantial gains in training and sampling efficiency over dense 3D generation, while our analyses characterize the remaining trade-offs in diversity and energetic relaxation. As an additional capability, intermediate occupancy states and selected spatial regions can condition the same generator without task-specific retraining. We use this interface for multiscale shape conditioning, regional editing, linker generation, and shape interpolation, extending the framework to spatially conditioned generation. Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce a multiscale octree flow framework that models small molecules as sparse typed point clouds and resolves their spatial structure from coarse to fine. • We formulate atom-level generation as a coarse-to-fine flow-denoising problem with continuous in-cell coordinates, combining hierarchical spatial organization with chemically precise local geometry; optional cell corruption improves robustness to generated spatial support. • We evaluate this representation through molecular quality, geometric and energetic analyses, and spatial conditioning, together with applications to shape-conditioned generation and regional editing. These studies characterize both its utility and its limitations. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

3D molecular generation. Early diffusion approaches established direct generation of three-dimensional molecules by jointly modeling chemical identity and atomic coordinates. EDM denoises atom features and coordinates with an equivariant network (Hoogeboom et al., 2022), and GeoLDM performs diffusion in a learned equivariant latent space (Xu et al., 2023). Subsequent methods extend this formulation with additional molecular structure or supervision: MiDi combines discrete graphs with continuous coordinates in a mixed diffusion process (Vignac et al., 2023), while UniGEM connects molecular generation with property prediction (Feng et al., 2025). These coordinate-based models typically sample the atom count before generating a fixed-size molecular state, whereas Jump Diffusion evolves molecular content and dimensionality within the same process (Campbell et al., 2023). VoxMol takes a spatial-field view by generating molecular densities on a dense voxel grid (Pinheiro et al., 2023). Flow matching provided an alternative to diffusion by learning transport vector fields (Lipman et al., 2023). SemlaFlow applies equivariant flows jointly to coordinates and chemical attributes, whereas TABASCO uses a non-equivariant transformer and reconstructs bonds afterward (Irwin et al., 2025; Vonessen et al., 2025). Zatom-1 extends multimodal flows with auxiliary predictive tasks, and Branching Flows supports changes in sample size through split and deletion events (Morehead et al., 2026; Billera et al., 2025). These atom- and graph-based methods generally lack explicit multiscale spatial support. Uni-3DAR autoregressively generates octree structural and atomic tokens (Lu et al., 2025); OctMole instead uses stage-wise occupancy flows over octree depths, followed by a joint flow for leaf-level atom types and continuous subcell coordinates. This preserves continuous geometry while exposing intermediate spatial scales for conditioning. 

Sparse and hierarchical 3D generation. Octrees have long provided a compact representation of 3D objects. Octree Transformer generates shapes autoregressively through hierarchically structured sequences (Ibing et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2025), while OctFusion uses octree-based diffusion for 3D shape generation (Xiong et al., 2024). TRELLIS generates sparse structure followed by features in structured 3D latents, supporting several output representations (Xiang et al., 2025). These methods 2establish important precedents for hierarchical generation and the separation of spatial support from its associated features. OctMole brings these ideas to molecular generation through a hierarchical occupancy flow: stages remain sequential across octree depths, while cells within each stage interact through joint denoising. 

Geometry-conditioned molecular design. Geometry has been incorporated through several com-plementary interfaces: ShapeMol conditions equivariant diffusion on molecular surfaces, UniGuide provides a general sampling-guidance framework, and ShEPhERD jointly models shape, electro-statics, and pharmacophores (Chen et al., 2023; Ayadi et al., 2024; Adams et al., 2025). DiffLinker instead conditions linker generation on molecular fragments and their spatial arrangement (Igashov et al., 2024). OctMole reuses the same generator with intermediate occupancy states or selected leaf regions, letting depth and region determine the resolution and extent of spatial control. We evaluate condition satisfaction and coordinate-frame sensitivity without assuming exact equivariance.

For a reference molecule, we extract its occupied cells at a chosen depth dm and resume the remaining occupancy transitions in Eq. (2), followed by the atom flow. Finer starting states leave fewer spatial decisions; at depth D, only atom types and local features are sampled. For regional editing, we retain selected finest cells, regrow the remaining support from coarse cells, and combine both supports before atom generation. Because these conditions fix spatial support rather than atom identities, they provide resolution- and region-based control without task-specific models, but do not guarantee preserved fragments or exact final geometry. We evaluate unconditional molecular generation, continuous geometric quality, and the spatial control exposed by intermediate octree states. 4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets and models. We train separate models on QM9 (Ramakrishnan et al., 2014) and GEOM-DRUG (Axelrod & G´ omez-Bombarelli, 2022), covering small organic molecules and larger, flexible drug-like molecules. Unless stated otherwise, all experiments use maximum octree depth D = 6 ,depth increment k = 2 , and 50-step Heun sampling. Main results use cell corruption on GEOM-DRUG and no corruption on QM9. Dataset, architecture, training, and sampling details are provided in Sections A and B. 

Baselines and sample selection. We compare with EDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2022), GeoLDM (Xu et al., 2023), UniGEM (Feng et al., 2025), and Uni-3DAR (Lu et al., 2025); UniGEM uses additional molecular properties during training. All unconditional results use 10,000 final samples per model under a shared chemical assessment. OctMole retains approximately 10% of its QM9 candidates and 20% of its GEOM-DRUG candidates by mean atom-type log probability, as does Uni-3DAR under its native protocol; the other baselines do not rerank. 

Metrics. We infer bonds with Open Babel (O’Boyle et al., 2011) and perform chemical checks with RDKit. Molecule stability requires every atom to have an allowed valency; validity measures successful sanitization of the largest fragment; connectedness is measured before fragment selection; and uniqueness is the fraction of distinct canonical SMILES among valid samples. We compare molecular size, atom and bond types, valencies, bond lengths, and bond angles against reference distributions using total variation or Wasserstein-1 distance. ## 5 ABLATIONS 

Octree versus dense generation. We replaced the hierarchical occupancy flow with flow matching on a dense 64 3 grid, using a DiT trained with the same number of epochs and GPUs. Table 4 summarizes this system-level comparison. The octree occupancy model reduces observed training time by at least 5× and sampling time by at least 4× on both datasets. It also achieves significantly better molecule stability. At higher resolutions, dense grids grow cubically while octree token counts grow roughly linearly, demonstrating the efficiency of our method. Full configurations, quality metrics, and resolution scaling are provided in Section E.1. 

Cell corruption. Cell corruption targets the support mismatch between atom-flow training and inference. Under generated support, it raises GEOM-DRUG molecule stability from 71.15% to 86.82% and connectedness from 90.60% to 96.90% (Table 5). The effect is weaker on QM9, where scores are near saturation. Separate GT-support tests show negligible changes, indicating that corruption primarily improves robustness to occupancy errors rather than clean support. 

Continuous offsets and quantization. Snapping OctMole-generated atoms to cell centers while fixing support and atom types increases GEOM-DRUG bond-length W1 from 0.017 to 0.135 ˚ Aand bond-angle W1 from 1.12 to 7.97 degrees. Directly quantizing 300 validation conformers shows the same grid effect (Fig. 13): at 64 3 resolution, quantization alone induces a median GFN2-xTB relaxation of 20.9 kcal/mol/atom, whereas OctMole with learned continuous offsets yields 0.63 kcal/mol/atom. The complete bond-geometry diagnostic is provided in Section E.2.

## 6 APPLICATIONS 

All applications reuse the same trained models without task-specific fine-tuning. Conditions act only on spatial support; atom types and coordinates are always generated. 

Shape-conditioned ligand reconstruction and exploration. We condition on the occupancy of 354 eligible PDBBind ligands and generate 20 candidates per ligand. Finest depth-6 occupancy nearly determines the ligand: 32.2% of queries have a candidate with Morgan-fingerprint Tanimoto similarity ≥ 0.99 . Coarser depth-4 occupancy instead retains shape while exploring chemistry: mean Chamfer distance is 0.840 ˚ A, mean Tanimoto similarity is 0.107, and 69.6% of candidates are valid, within 1 ˚ A of the query, and have similarity at most 0.3; 297 of 354 queries yield at least one such mimic. Table 6 and Fig. 5 summarize this trade-off. No pocket information is supplied, so shape matching does not imply preserved binding; full details are in Section F.1. 

Regional editing and linker generation. Retaining one spatial region while regrowing another, or fixing two end regions while generating a linker, demonstrates that the spatial interface extends beyond global shape conditioning. Both tasks also reveal that joining independently generated supports remains a principal failure mode. Figure 6 demonstrates a linker generation case. Full protocols, quantitative results, and examples are provided in Sections F.2 and F.3. 

Shape interpolation. Interpolating depth-4 occupancy fields yields consistent shape transitions across 20 reference pairs: 59 of 60 selected intermediate outputs are valid and 53 are molecule-stable. ## 7 CONCLUSION 

Main conclusions. We introduced OctMole, a multiscale framework that first generates sparse octree occupancy from coarse to fine and then jointly generates atom types and continuous in-cell coordinates. On GEOM-DRUG, OctMole achieves 86.82% molecule stability and 96.90% connectedness, outperforms Uni-3DAR on five of six distributional distances, remains the most stable generator across atom-count bins, and attains a 94.86% end-to-end xTB yield. On QM9, its median relaxation RMSD of 0.0628 ˚ A is close to the 0.0574 ˚ A data reference. The same hierarchy also exposes spatial control without task-specific retraining: supplied occupancies reach 0.97–1.00 IoU, and the joint shape-and-chemistry success rate peaks at 53.3% for depth-4 GEOM-DRUG conditioning and 41.3% for depth-2 QM9 conditioning. Regional editing, linker generation, and shape interpolation further demonstrate this interface. 

Limitations and scope. Several limitations qualify these results. First, QM9 uniqueness is only 80.77%, and the remaining relationship between repetition and training-set memorization requires molecule-level analysis. Second, octree conditions are tied to a coordinate frame: on GEOM-DRUG, depth-4 molecule stability falls from 95.40% to 82.95% under rotation and to 80.05% when rotation and grid-origin shifts are combined. Third, OctMole does not generate bonds explicitly; bonds are inferred from atom types and coordinates using Open Babel and RDKit, making chemical metrics dependent on this inference pipeline. Finally, xTB energy and RMSD statistics include only valid, connected samples whose optimizations converge. Reporting end-to-end yield exposes the size of this subset but does not remove survivor bias within it. 

Future directions. We follow the preprocessing and data splits of EDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2022) for both benchmarks. 

QM9. QM9 (Ramakrishnan et al., 2014) contains small organic molecules with up to nine heavy atoms. Starting from approximately 134K molecules, the EDM preprocessing removes uncharacter-ized entries and retains one lowest-energy conformation per molecule, leaving 130,831 molecules split into 100,000 for training, 17,748 for validation, and 13,083 for testing. Molecules contain at most 29 atoms when hydrogens are included. 

GEOM-DRUG. GEOM-DRUG (Axelrod & G´ omez-Bombarelli, 2022) contains larger and more flexible drug-like molecules. Starting from approximately 304K molecules, the EDM preprocessing retains up to the 30 lowest-energy conformations per molecule, yielding roughly six million conformer records. Our processed data contain 5,538,014 training and 692,251 validation records. Molecules have 44.2 atoms on average and up to 181 atoms. Both datasets include explicit hydrogen atoms.

B.1 DATA PREPROCESSING 

We embed each molecule in a cubic domain of side length B, using B = 15 .36 ˚A for QM9 and

## C EVALUATION PROTOCOLS

The dense and octree systems use the same final 64 3 resolution, number of epochs, GPUs, and 50-step Heun sampling within each dataset. Their spatial generators, parameter counts, effective batch sizes, and architectures differ; the comparison therefore measures complete systems rather than sparsity in isolation. Tables 13 and 14 report the corresponding training and sampling measurements. For inference, each method retains its complete candidate-generation and reranking procedure to produce approximately 1,000 final molecules on one GPU. Timing excludes subsequent Open Babel evaluation. From 64 3 to 256 3, dense grids grow by 64 ×, whereas mean octree node counts grow by only 1.59 ×

on GEOM-DRUG and 1.43 × on QM9. Measured occupancy-flow sampling from depth 6 to 8 on GEOM-DRUG grows from 0.079 to 0.110 seconds per molecule and from 691 to 776 MB peak memory. E.3 HIERARCHY SETTINGS 

We compare k = 1 and k = 2 at fixed finest depth D = 6 , using six and three occupancy stages, respectively. We additionally evaluate D = 8 , k = 2 on GEOM-DRUG. The atom model and 50-step sampling method are fixed within each dataset. At D = 6 , k = 1 and k = 2 have comparable chemical quality, while k = 2 halves the number of stages. Increasing to D = 8 without retuning substantially degrades the GEOM-DRUG result (Table 15). E.4 LOCAL PERTURBATIONS ACROSS DEPTHS 

We project 128 GEOM-DRUG references to depth d∗ ∈ { 2, 4} and compare deletion, shift, and pruning perturbations before regenerating deeper levels and running the atom flow. Each operator, control, and depth uses 256 samples (two per reference). To avoid confounding depth with physical perturbation strength, we shift depth-4 cells four grid steps and apply depth-2 deletion or pruning regions to every depth-4 cell they cover. Matched deletion and pruning reduce depth-2 IoU similarly at both injection depths, while matched depth-4 shifting is more destructive because many small cells move independently. Finer conditioning nevertheless preserves more unperturbed fine detail: after matched deletion, absolute depth-4 IoU is 0.68 when conditioning at depth 4 and 0.24 when conditioning at depth 2 (Fig. 14). E.5 SAMPLING STEPS AND CANDIDATE SELECTION 

We compare 50, 25, and 10 steps with fixed checkpoints and candidate budgets, retaining 500 samples per setting. Timings use one A100 40GB. OctMole timing covers both flows and excludes loading, sorting, file writing, and evaluation; the Uni-3DAR row uses its native generation-only protocol. Twenty-five steps are 1.77 × faster on QM9 and 1.89 × faster on GEOM-DRUG than 50 steps, with nearby scores in this pilot; ten steps degrade stability and connectedness. Because this experiment has only 500 final samples, it does not establish equal large-sample diversity, and the main evaluation retains 50 steps. Table 16 contains the complete results. QM9 is already saturated, ranging from 98.5% to 99.9% across deciles (Fig. 15).
